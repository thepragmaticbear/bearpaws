# Bearpaws v4.1.0: narrower money rule, three-review cap, first Opus benchmark

## Why 4.1.0

- **Fewer changes count as elevated.** "Money" now means moving money. Work that was elevated in 4.0.0 (price display, formatting, cart and discount arithmetic) is routine and gets no review.
- **Reviews are capped.** Outside subagent-driven development, a change gets at most three reviews.

## Moving money

- Elevated: charging, refunding, paying out, transferring, or writing balances. Routine: price display, formatting, and cart or discount arithmetic. Changed in `bp:using-bearpaws`, `bp:subagent-driven-development` and the diagnosis prompt.
- The hooks' path-name nudge follows: it flags `refund`, `payout`, `ledger` and the whole words `charge`, `charges`, `charged`, `chargeback`, and no longer flags `invoice`, `price`, `pricing` or `money`. It is a nudge and misses money code without these stems.
- On Opus, a cart-discount task went from $1.09 to $0.20 per accepted run (3.7 reviews to none). Cost of the change: before it, review switched that cart's rounding to half-up in 3 of 3 runs; after it, no run is reviewed.

## Three-review cap

- At most three reviews per change, redone and scoped re-reviews included. After the third, edit nothing more: an invalid third review, or an unfixed Critical or Important finding listed in `Open:`, means `Status: INCOMPLETE`. A confirmed defect in changed code in the task's elevated risk class counts as at least Important, even if the reviewer calls it Minor. Subagent-driven development follows the same finding rule and keeps its five-round cap per task.
- The rule is text in the bootstrap and `bp:requesting-code-review`; no hook counts reviews. In 48 live Opus runs no run exceeded three reviews or edited source after the third (before the cap was in the bootstrap, up to 9).

## Opus benchmark

- First full-session benchmark on Opus: [docs/benchmarks/2026-11-18-opus-baseline.md](../../benchmarks/2026-11-18-opus-baseline.md), with rows beside it.
- v4.0.0 cost 3.3 times no plugin per accepted task on the default suite and 6.3 times on the risk suite. With the narrower money rule and review cap, Bearpaws cost 1.9 and 4.1 times as much in the first comparison and about 1.8 and 4.2 times as much in the later four-arm run.
- A no-plugin arm given only a two-sentence note asking for an independent review of risky changes matched the plugin on escaped defects (3 of 18 each, no plugin 8) at about 40% lower cost per accepted risk task, with 16 of 18 runs accepted against 18.
- A bootstrap cut to 3,396 bytes was tested and not adopted: it stopped asking on vague requests (0 of 15 against 5 of 15) and let more authorization defects through (9 of 13 against 3 of 13, pooled after the fact).
- The text-only routing eval scored that bootstrap 27/27 on the behavior it then failed live. It records stated intent; do not use it alone to justify bootstrap changes.

## Tests

- Routing eval: three cases for the money rule (`cart-discount`, `price-format`, `refund-cap`), a `*` route that accepts only `probe`, `patch` or `build`.
- Hook tests cover the new path stems in both gates.
